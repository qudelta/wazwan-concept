Key training terms in an ANN (MLP) · 1 of 3

How long do we train?

1

Epoch

One epoch: every training row is used once.

Definition

An epoch is one complete pass through the entire training dataset by the model.

Analogy

Like reading the whole book once.

In practice
  • We train for many epochs (10, 50, 100, …) so the model keeps improving.
  • Within each epoch the weights are updated again and again, once per batch.
AI & ML for Civil EngineersSession 06
Part 01 · ANN intuition19 / 43
Key training terms in an ANN (MLP) · 2 of 3

How much data per update?

2

Batch size

Example: 800 rows, batch size 32 → 25 weight updates per epoch.

Definition

The number of training samples the model processes before updating the weights once.

Why it matters
  • The model does not always look at the whole dataset at once.
  • It breaks the data into smaller batches. Common values: 32, 64, 128.
In practice
  • Small batch: more updates, noisier learning, but faster to start.
  • Large batch: smoother updates, more memory.

Part 01 · ANN intuition20 / 43
Key training terms in an ANN (MLP) · 3 of 3

How big is each step?

3

Learning rate

Each update moves the weights a step downhill on the loss. The learning rate sets the step size.

Definition

A small number that controls how big a step the model takes while updating the weights during training.

Why it matters
  • Too high: the model may overshoot the optimum and not converge.
  • Too low: the model learns very slowly and may get stuck.
Analogy

Like your pace while hiking towards a goal: not too fast, not too slow.

How does an ANN learn?

Adjust weights and biases to reduce a loss

1

Forward pass

The inputs flow through the layers and produce a prediction ŷ.

2

Calculate loss

Compare ŷ with the observed strength y: squared error for regression.

3

Backpropagate

Compute the gradient of the loss for every weight and bias.

4

Update

The optimiser (Adam by default) takes a step of size set by the learning rate.

Repeat for every batch, epoch after epoch, until the loss stops improving.
This is optimisation, the same gradient descent as Session 03. It is not the discovery of a guaranteed physical law.

Training loss and validation score

They answer different questions

What the notebook’s training plot shows

Early stopping keeps the iteration with the best validation score.
Training loss

Tracks the optimisation objective on training batches. It is not the test RMSE.

Internal validation R²

Performance on a held-out part of the training data, used by early stopping.

  • A falling training loss is not evidence of generalisation.
  • Inspect both curves; note if training hit the iteration limit.
  • The internal validation subset is not the final test set.

How do we measure regression performance?

Complementary metrics, not one number

Mean absolute error

MAE

mean |y − ŷ|

The average size of an error, in MPa.

Root mean squared error

RMSE

√ mean (y − ŷ)²

Also in MPa; penalises large errors more strongly.

Coefficient of determination

R²

1 − SSres / SStot

Compared with always predicting the mean; can be negative.

e = y − ŷ

Does an ANN understand concrete?

No: it learns a statistical mapping from examples

  • InterpolationPrediction in a region reasonably represented by the training data.
  • ExtrapolationPrediction beyond the domain of the training data: particularly risky.
  • Physical validityNot established by a high test score alone.
  • DeploymentNeeds representative validation, plausible behaviour, and attention to the consequences of errors.
The training domain
Cement102–540 kg/m³
Water122–247 kg/m³
Superplasticizer0.0–32.2 kg/m³
Age1–365 days
Strength2.3–82.6 MPa

A mix outside these ranges, or an unusual combination inside them, is extrapolation.

Take-home messages

ANNs are useful tools, not automatic upgrades

1Understand the mappingInputs → weighted combinations → nonlinear activations → output.
2Training is optimisationLoss, gradients and parameter updates shape the learned model.
3Validate correctlyValidation data for choices; the test set only for the final evaluation.
4Compare honestlyHere, the random forest beat the selected ANN on the held-out test set.
5Keep claims proportionalScores do not establish causation, physical validity or safe extrapolation.
The central lesson
Choose the model the evidence supports, not the most sophisticated one.
